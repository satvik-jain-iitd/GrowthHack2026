# Resy Guide to Stripe Disputes

**It’s important to always respond to any disputes**from guests in your Stripe portal to maintain a good standing in your Stripe account. If you don't respond to a dispute within the provided timeline, it will result in an automatic loss. Stripe may place restrictions on your Stripe account if your dispute levels exceed certain thresholds that result in guests being unable to book any paid reservations at your restaurant.

## Understanding disputes

A dispute (or chargeback) occurs when a guest questions a charge made at your business with their card issuer. When a dispute is initiated, your Stripe account will be debited the original charge amount, plus a dispute fee, which is generated by the card network. The dispute fee for your country can be found on the [Stripe pricing page](https://stripe.com/pricing). If you win the dispute, the disputed funds will be returned to your Stripe account, but the dispute fee will not. Because of this, dispute prevention is key.

There can be many reasons for your guest(s) to initiate a dispute, including but not limited to:

* The guest didn’t recognize the charge
* The guest didn’t understand the cancellation/no-show policy
* A stolen card was used for a fraudulent transaction
* The guest attempted cancellation within the acceptable time frame but was unsuccessful
* The guest doesn’t agree with the validity of the charge
* The guest is unhappy with the service or product offered

There is a dispute resolution process, outlined below, in which you can respond and submit evidence to demonstrate that the payment was valid. You can read more about how disputes work [here](https://stripe.com/docs/disputes/how-disputes-work).

**Please note:** The dispute process can take up to 2-3 months to complete after you’ve submitted evidence to challenge the dispute. Please see [here](https://stripe.com/docs/disputes/how-disputes-work#timing) for more information on dispute timelines.

  

## Preventing disputes

With disputes, **prevention is the highest priority**. While winning disputes is important, preventing them from occurring in the first place is the only thing that will reduce your dispute rate. As part of your financial obligations to card networks, you must ensure that disputes are kept at acceptable levels. If these exceed the thresholds dictated by each network (for example, [Visa](https://stripe.com/docs/disputes/monitoring-programs#visa-programs) or [Mastercard](https://stripe.com/docs/disputes/monitoring-programs#mastercard-programs)), they place your account into one of their [monitoring programs](https://stripe.com/docs/disputes/monitoring-programs). 

**Separately, Stripe may place restrictions on your Stripe account if your dispute levels exceed certain thresholds.** Read further below for what to do if your Stripe account becomes rejected.

### Include a Double Confirmation message

We recommend including a Double Confirmation message that pops up when guests book specific tables or all reservations. This pop-up message can contain important information that you'd like guests to confirm that they've read before they can complete booking their reservation.  Learn how to add one [here](https://helpdesk.resy.com/en_us/double-confirmation-message-B1Km8K46F). 

Make sure to write out your cancellation policy in clear, precise language. You must clearly state the amount of the cancellation fee and how far in advance prior to a reservation a guest must cancel without being charged a fee. Consider including a brief explanation as to why you are implementing a cancellation policy. It may also be beneficial to include an explanation of what will happen if your guest does not show up or is considerably late. 

### Use a statement descriptor that's quickly recognizable

Guests will sometimes dispute charges because they don’t understand what the charge is for when they review the card account statement. To help reduce this confusion, make sure your statement descriptor is set up to reflect your business name (and if needed, your location or other info that will help guests recognize what the charge is for).

You can set your statement descriptor by logging into your Stripe account and navigating to Settings > Public Details, or by clicking [here](https://dashboard.stripe.com/settings/public). You can read more about how statement descriptors work [here](https://stripe.com/docs/payments/account/statement-descriptors).

### Consider contacting guests with large reservations or pre-payments

For reservations that could incur a large cancellation fee, or large pre-payment charges, consider emailing, calling, or texting the guest to ensure that the reservation is accurate and that they’re aware of your cancellation policy.

For phone calls, you can share call logs as evidence when fighting a dispute. But email conversations and texts are typically much quicker to submit and generally seen as stronger evidence by dispute reviewers. For those reasons, we recommend emailing or texting over calling when possible.

## Knowing when a guest has filed a dispute

When a guest disputes a charge with their bank, Stripe will notify you of the dispute in the Stripe Dashboard and via email. It’s important to know right away when a dispute has been filed, so we recommend the following:

* Since Stripe will send you an email whenever a dispute is filed, make sure that your Stripe account is associated with an email address that you regularly check
* Log in to your Stripe account on a regular basis, and navigate to Payments > Disputes (or click [here](https://dashboard.stripe.com/disputes)) to review any new or ongoing disputes

**Please note:** You're responsible for responding to all disputes within your Stripe dashboard. If you don't respond to a dispute within the provided timeline, it'll result in an automatic loss.

## Responding to disputes

### Attempt to contact the guest to resolve

Oftentimes, the best way to resolve a dispute is to contact the guest directly. This allows you to:

* Better understand why they initiated the dispute
* Determine if there's an alternative solution
* Creates a record of you attempting to resolve the situation
* Understand any customer service or product related issues associated with the guest's experience
* If you can find a solution or workaround with your guest, it can allow you to continue doing business together and avoid a potential dispute loss. Some examples of common outcomes from this type of outreach, if you can agree to a resolution with your guest, are:
* Guests may claim they called to cancel the reservation within the specified time frame, but weren’t able to reach anyone
* The guest forgot they agreed to a cancellation policy when they booked, or forgot about the reservation, and are confused about the charge
* The guest may wish to set up a new reservation and avoid the cancellation fees

Many disputes are resolved with guests directly through these conversations. If the guest agrees the charge is valid, or you’re able to reach an alternative solution, **request that they contact their bank and withdraw the dispute.**

After speaking to the guest, regardless of outcome, respond to the dispute in the Stripe dashboard with this additional communication included in the evidence.

### Counter a dispute

If you'd like to challenge the dispute and submit supporting evidence, you can do so when reviewing the disputed payment in the [Stripe dashboard](https://dashboard.stripe.com/disputes). Stripe will present a summary of the dispute, along with suggested next steps, deadline information, and options to either accept the dispute or counter it:

[[IMAGE: guide-to-stripe-disputes__img01]]  

Selecting the “Counter dispute” option will take you to an evidence submission form, which collects information to better help you counter the guest’s claim. **Please make sure to fill out this section accurately and provide a good description of the product or service.**

**Please note:** It’s important to understand that card issuers review thousands of dispute responses every day. Writing a long or personal explanation to them isn’t going to make your responses more convincing. Instead, describe clearly and concisely why the claim is unreasonable or incorrect, and how your evidence proves that, using a neutral and professional tone.

**Good Example:** Jenny Rosen booked a reservation with our company on [date] using a [Visa] credit card. We did not receive a cancellation of this reservation, and also sent two different confirmation emails prior to the date of our reservation (included below). Per our Terms and Conditions and the agreed to cancelation policy (included below), we charged [$amount] per person to cover the reservation fee.

**Clear narrative of the event - Unemotional and to the point- Information supported by evidence**

**Poor Example:**Jenny Rosen is a scammer and a thief. She used her credit card to book a reservation and then never came in. We believe she did this to hurt our business and wrote bad reviews also. We believe she did this maliciously even though we made an effort to resolve.

**Emotional and aggressive language - Unprovable assertions regarding the cardholder’s motive**

Once you’ve provided your description and filled in the other information, you can proceed to the evidence submission page. This page will allow you to upload screenshots, or other file types, as pieces of evidence. Once uploaded, you can select the type of evidence from the dropdown menu.

### Evidence best practices

Evidence should be clear, organized, and relevant. Present your evidence in a clear format. For cancellation policy violations, consider submitting evidence chronologically. Start with evidence showing when the reservation was booked, what date/time was chosen, when the cancellation was requested, then any relevant communication that took place thereafter.

Examples of compelling evidence include (but are not limited to):

* A copy of your cancellation or refund policy using screenshots of the booking flow showing the terms that the guest agreed to and your double confirmation message
* Screenshots showing exactly when the guest made the reservation, the date/time of the reservation, and exactly when the guest cancelled
* Any additional communication you may have had with the guest, including email exchanges or notes from phone calls. Ensure you capture the guest’s contact information in your screenshots for evidence

## Frequently asked questions

Q: Should I always charge cancellation fees?

A: It is your right as a business owner to present these fees for no show or late cancellation. However, working with the guest directly to find a solution may be in your best interest. Some restaurants offer to put the cancellation fee toward another meal to avoid disputes. This encourages long-term relationships and good word of mouth from your guests. Guests that have initiated a chargeback/dispute against an establishment will not often return for future reservations and business.

  

Q: What should I put in my "description" when fighting my dispute?

A: Focus on the provable facts that you can back up with evidence and be clear and to the point. If the guest violated your cancellation policy, then describe the policy terms, when they were shown and agreed to by the guest, when the guest booked the reservation, and when they cancelled it. Keep your submissions succinct and fact-based.

  

Q: How long after a charge can a guest file a dispute?

A: Card networks typically allow cardholders to initiate disputes within 120 days of the original payment, but their rules allow more time in some situations. Certain industries, such as travel or event ticketing—where the payment might be made long before the event occurs—are prone to longer time intervals between the original purchase and a dispute. Generally speaking, when a guest makes a payment for something that will happen in the future (like a vacation reservation, a professional services appointment, or an event ticket), the clock starts on the date of the chargeback event, not the date of the payment.

## What to do if your Stripe account has been rejected due to an elevated dispute rate

Stripe will always notify you before rejecting your account due to elevated disputes, so make sure you’re regularly reviewing emails from Stripe.

If you receive a notice, or you believe your Stripe account has been rejected due to excessive disputes, please contact the Resy support team immediately at [resysupport@resy.com](mailto:resysupport@resy.com) with the subject line: “Stripe account rejected”.
